# Can I get FoodShare as a student?

You may be able to. Being in school does not automatically lock you out. FoodShare is Wisconsin's name for SNAP, the program that helps you buy groceries (it loads onto a Wisconsin QUEST card, like a debit card for food). Whether you may qualify depends on your income, your household size, and a few student-specific rules below.

Start here: if you're enrolled half-time or more, you usually qualify by meeting an "exemption" — a specific situation (like your work hours, work-study, caring for a child, or your age) that lets a half-time-or-more student get FoodShare. (If you're enrolled less than half-time, this part doesn't apply to you — see below.) Your screener will ask about your situation to check whether an exemption fits you.

A few things to know:

- If you go to school half-time or more, you must meet an exemption AND meet all the regular FoodShare rules. Your school decides what counts as "half-time," not us.
- If you go less than half-time, the special student rules do not apply to you. You're treated like any other applicant.
- If you're in a program outside the regular degree curriculum — such as remedial classes, ESOL/ESL (English language) classes, continuing or community education, professional development, or workforce development — you don't need to meet a student exemption.
- Being claimed as a dependent on someone's taxes does NOT decide this. You can still qualify even if a parent claims you on their tax return.

You don't have to figure this out alone. Your screener estimate can give you a sense of where you stand and which exemptions may apply to you.

Remember: the State of Wisconsin makes the final decision after you apply.

Source: WI DHS FoodShare Handbook (P-16001), Release 26-01, §3.15.1 Student Eligibility; SBNC WI Cohort FAQ
