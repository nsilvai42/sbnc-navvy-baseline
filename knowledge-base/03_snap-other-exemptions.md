# Other ways students can qualify for FoodShare

Working 20 hours a week is not the only way to qualify. Wisconsin (which calls SNAP "FoodShare") has several student pathways. If you are enrolled at least half-time, you may be eligible if **any one** of these fits you:

**Caregiving**
- You care for a child under age 6.
- You care for a child age 6 to 11 and adequate child care isn't available to let you attend class or your work activities.
- You are a single parent enrolled full-time in college and caring for a child under age 12.

**Your age**
- You are under 18, or you are 50 or older.

**Health**
- You are physically or mentally unfit for work because of a health condition (usually shown through disability benefits or a statement from a doctor or licensed psychologist).

**You already get TANF**
- You receive TANF (Temporary Assistance for Needy Families) cash assistance.

You only need to match one pathway, not all of them. And remember: if you are enrolled **less than** half-time, these student rules don't apply to you at all. Whether you qualify also depends on your income and household size. Want a quick read on where you might land? Try the screener estimate. The State of Wisconsin makes the final decision once you apply.

A note: being claimed on a parent's taxes does **not** block you. You can still qualify on your own if you meet a FoodShare pathway.

Source: WI DHS FoodShare Handbook (P-16001), Release 26-01, §3.15.1 Student Eligibility; USDA FNS SNAP Student Eligibility Guidance; SBNC WI Cohort FAQ
