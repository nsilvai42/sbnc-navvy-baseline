# What student-facing words mean (a quick glossary)

Here are the everyday words you'll run into while applying for FoodShare or BadgerCare Plus. Each is one plain line.

- **FoodShare** — Wisconsin's name for SNAP, the program that helps you buy groceries.
- **BadgerCare Plus** — Wisconsin's health-coverage program (its version of Medicaid).
- **EBT / QUEST card** — the "Wisconsin QUEST card" your FoodShare money loads onto; you swipe it like a debit card to buy food.
- **ACCESS** — the State of Wisconsin's online system where you apply for and manage these benefits.
- **Household** — the people counted together on your application. For FoodShare, this is usually people who live with you and buy and make food together. (A few people are always counted together — for example, a student under 22 living with a parent — even if they buy and make food separately.)
- **Gross income** — your income *before* taxes are taken out. FoodShare looks at this, then subtracts certain deductions.
- **Deduction** — an amount subtracted from your income before eligibility is figured, so it can lower the income that counts.
- **Half-time** — your enrollment level as your *school* defines it. Whether you're at least half-time can change which FoodShare student rules apply to you.
- **Exemption (student)** — a student-eligibility pathway that may let a student enrolled at least half-time still qualify for FoodShare (for example, through work, work-study, caregiving, age, or disability). This is different from FoodShare's separate work-requirement exemptions.
- **Work-study** — a financial-aid program that lets eligible students earn money through approved jobs; your financial-aid office can confirm if you have it.
- **TANF** — Temporary Assistance for Needy Families, a cash-assistance program. Getting it can matter for some benefit rules.
- **Estimate** — the result your screener gives you. It's a careful guess of what you may be eligible for, not a decision.
- **Recertification / renewal** — a later step to keep your benefits active after you're approved; it's usually not part of your first application.

Whether you may qualify depends on your income and household size. Your screener estimate is a careful guess, not a decision — the State of Wisconsin makes the final call after you apply.
