# Does applying for FoodShare affect my financial aid?

This is an important question, and we want to give you a reliable answer rather than a guess. The confirmed answer is in the box below — and you can always check with your school's financial-aid office, which can speak to how aid works for your specific situation.

Why students ask this: FoodShare and financial aid are run by different systems for different reasons. FoodShare (Wisconsin's name for SNAP food benefits) is a food-assistance program. Financial aid (grants, loans, work-study) comes through your school and the FAFSA. They are separate processes.

A few things that are safe to keep in mind:

- FoodShare benefits load onto a Wisconsin QUEST card (an EBT card, the card you swipe to buy groceries). That is separate from your student aid.
- Work-study can actually matter in your favor: taking part in a state or federally financed work-study program is one of the ways a half-time-or-more student may meet a FoodShare student exemption. (Your financial-aid office can confirm whether your job is work-study.)

If your situation feels complicated, the safest next step is to check the approved financial-aid source or talk to your school's financial-aid office — they can speak to how aid works for you specifically.
