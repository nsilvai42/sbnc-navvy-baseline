# How FoodShare counts income and household

Your "household" for FoodShare is the people you live with AND buy and prepare food with. Your income is counted before taxes, but FoodShare subtracts certain expenses (called deductions) before deciding. Whether you may qualify depends on your household size and income. Your screener estimate gives you a careful guess, and the State of Wisconsin makes the final decision after you apply.

Two pieces, in plain terms:

- Household. This is not just who lives in your place. It's who you live with and share food with — buying groceries together and cooking together. If you live with roommates but buy and prepare your food separately, you may be counted as a smaller household — sometimes just yourself. Some rules can still group certain people together no matter how they handle food, so the State decides your household size based on your answers. The application will ask about this, so answer based on how you actually handle food.

- Income. FoodShare starts with your "gross" income (money you earn before taxes are taken out) — things like wages, self-employment, or unemployment. It then applies deductions for certain costs, so the number compared against the income guidelines for your household size is usually lower than your paycheck total.

Working students count too. Having a job or earning a low wage does not automatically rule you out. Many people who get FoodShare have jobs, and working can even help some students meet a student rule. The amount and your situation are what matter, not the fact that you work.

A common mix-up: "who lives together" is not the same as "who buys and prepares food together." Getting that right on your application matters because it affects both eligibility and your benefit amount.

Want a quick read on your situation? Use the screener for an estimate, then apply through ACCESS (Wisconsin's online benefits portal) so a Wisconsin agency — your county or tribal office — can make the official decision.
