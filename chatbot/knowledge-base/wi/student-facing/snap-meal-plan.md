# Living on campus with a meal plan?

Having a campus meal plan does not automatically rule you out of FoodShare (Wisconsin's name for SNAP, the program that helps you buy groceries). The key question is how many of your meals the plan covers.

Here is the rule:

- If you live in campus housing AND your meal plan covers **more than half** of your meals, Wisconsin treats you as an "institutional resident" (someone whose meals are already provided). In that case, you are **not eligible** for FoodShare.
- If your plan covers **half or fewer** of your meals, this rule does **not** by itself block you. You may still be eligible if you meet the other student rules.

**A quick example.** A typical full meal plan is 21 meals a week (3 a day). Wisconsin's handbook uses this math:

- A plan covering **11 of 21** meals = about 52% (more than half) -> not eligible.
- A plan covering **10 of 21** meals = about 48% (less than half) -> does not, on its own, block you.

So a smaller "block" or "flex" plan that covers only some meals is usually fine.

**If your meal plan is hard to count** (for example, a set number of swipes, points, or dollars), the agency figures the share with you — usually based on the meals your plan *makes available*, not how many you actually eat (meals you paid for but skip still count). If it's unclear, they may check with you or your school.

You do not have to figure this out perfectly on your own. Your Navvy screener estimate can help, and the State of Wisconsin makes the final decision after you apply. When you apply or do your interview, just answer honestly about whether you live on campus and roughly how many meals your plan covers.

Source: WI DHS FoodShare Handbook (P-16001), 3.15.1.3 Student Institutional Meal Plans; SBNC WI FoodShare Guide
