# Working or in work-study? You may meet the student rule

If you go to college at least half-time, there's an extra "student rule" for FoodShare (Wisconsin's name for SNAP, monthly grocery money on a QUEST card). The good news: working or doing work-study is one of the most common ways students meet it. If one of these fits you, you may be eligible, as long as you also meet the regular income and household rules.

You may meet the student rule if you:

- Work at least 20 hours a week in paid work. This can be one job or a few jobs added together. If you're self-employed, Wisconsin uses a monthly average — about 80 hours a month — with earnings (after business expenses) that come out to at least the federal minimum wage. Because it's a monthly average, one slow week won't disqualify you.
- Are in a state or federally funded work-study program during the school year. You only need to be approved for work-study; you don't have to have worked the hours yet. Your financial-aid office can confirm this.
- Are in a paid on-the-job training program (you learn while you get paid). This one counts only while your employer is actively training you.
- Were assigned or placed into your college by a workforce or training program — such as SNAP Employment and Training (FSET), the Workforce Innovation and Opportunity Act (WIOA), or Trade Adjustment Assistance (TAA). (This is about being placed into school by the program, not just being enrolled while you happen to be in one.)

Quick self-check: Do you work close to 20 hours a week, or have a work-study award? If yes, mention it when you apply. If your hours go up and down, the state usually looks at your monthly average (about 80 hours a month).

You don't enter your work hours here. The State of Wisconsin makes the final decision after you apply through ACCESS (Wisconsin's online benefits application system at access.wi.gov) and finish your interview. Your screener result is just an estimate.
