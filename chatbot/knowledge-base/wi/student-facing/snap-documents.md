# What documents you will need for FoodShare

You can start your FoodShare application now. You do not need every document in hand first. FoodShare is Wisconsin's name for SNAP, the program that helps you buy groceries. The State of Wisconsin may ask for some documents after you apply, so it helps to know what kinds of things might come up.

Have these categories ready if you can:

- **Identity** — something that shows who you are.
- **Wisconsin residence** — something that shows you live in Wisconsin.
- **Income** — something that shows what you earn, like a recent pay stub.
- **School or student status** — something that shows your enrollment, like a class schedule (your school decides what counts as full-time or half-time).
- **Proof of your work-study award** (for example, your financial-aid award letter) — only if you're using work-study to meet a student exemption. Your financial-aid office can help you get this.
- **Household and expenses** — basic info about who you live with and costs like rent.

Missing something? Keep going anyway. Each document just needs to *show* one of these facts, and you can usually track it down or request it later. Don't let a missing pay stub stop you from starting.

After you submit, you must complete an interview with the State of Wisconsin — this is a required step, so don't skip it. They may also request documents before they send your decision notice. Watch your phone, email, and mail so you don't miss the interview or any requests. The State of Wisconsin makes the final decision on your application.

A safety note: have these items nearby for your own records, but do not type sensitive details like your Social Security number or exact income into this chat.
